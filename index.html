The privilege trap

Most consumer AI tools (ChatGPT free/Plus, Gemini free, Claude free) retain user inputs by default and train on them. Never paste client documents into them. The tools above explicitly contract around this: zero retention, SOC 2, and contractual privilege protection. If your firm's IT team hasn't signed off on a tool, don't put client data in it.

The traditional research platforms (LexisNexis, Westlaw) added AI layers. The newer entrants (Casetext, vLex) build AI-first and integrate the databases underneath. The trade-off: incumbents have deeper case coverage; newcomers have better interfaces and lower prices.

The free prompt pack (DIY route)

If you want to do it yourself with ChatGPT Plus ($20/mo) — and only for non-client-facing work, never with privileged documents — here's the 6-prompt system we use to draft contract language and motion sections that beat most first-draft associate work. Save these in a Notion doc.

  1. Frame the deal — "I'm drafting a services agreement between [Party A] (US-based SaaS company) and [Party B] (EU enterprise customer). The deal is $240K/year, 24-month term. List the 8 clauses that will be most heavily negotiated, in priority order."
  2. Draft the MSA shell — "Using the 8 priority clauses above, generate the first draft of a master services agreement. Use plain-English drafting style. Include defined terms with quotation marks on first use. End with a signature block."
  3. Generate a clause library — "For each of the following clauses, generate 3 alternative versions: limitation of liability, indemnification, IP assignment, data processing (GDPR-style), termination for convenience, force majeure. Tag each alternative as 'favorable to seller,' 'balanced,' or 'favorable to buyer.'"
  4. Motion section first draft — "I'm opposing a motion to dismiss in [jurisdiction]. The cause of action is [X]. The defendant's argument is [Y]. Draft the first 3 pages of my opposition brief: introduction, statement of facts, and the first argument heading. Use IRAC. No fabricated citations."
  5. Client email from notes — "Convert these rough notes into a 200-word client email: [paste notes]. Tone: warm but not saccharine. End with one clear ask (decision or response needed) and a date by which."
  6. Objection-handling FAQ — "Generate the 5 questions opposing counsel will ask about [motion/brief topic]. For each, draft a one-paragraph response I can rehearse in deposition or hearing prep."

The total time per draft: ~25 minutes for a first pass. Quality: useful for non-privileged work only — never for documents that contain client confidential information unless your firm has signed an enterprise agreement with the AI provider.

Questions lawyers actually ask

What is the single best AI tool for a lawyer just starting out?
A drafting assistant like Spellbook plus a research tool like Casetext. One replaces 3-5 hours of contract or motion drafting; the other cuts research time by 40-60%. Together they're roughly $165/mo per seat — cheaper than the LexisNexis subscription you'd otherwise need.
Are these AI tools safe for privileged client data?
The serious ones (Spellbook, CoCounsel, Lexis+ AI) have explicit confidentiality terms, SOC 2 Type II, and zero-retention policies you can show your malpractice carrier. Generic consumer AI (ChatGPT free/Plus, Claude free, Gemini free) does not — those services retain user inputs and may use them for training. Never paste client documents into consumer AI tools unless you have a signed enterprise agreement that overrides the default terms.
Do these tools actually replace junior associates?
No — and any vendor that tells you they do is selling something. They replace the 4 hours of grunt work (first-pass draft, cite-check, doc review, dep summary) that a junior associate used to do before the interesting 4 hours. The interesting 4 hours (strategy, judgment, client relationship) still need a lawyer. What changes is that one associate can do the work that used to require two.
Will AI put lawyers out of work?
It will put lawyers who refuse to use AI at a 30-40% efficiency disadvantage against lawyers who do. That disadvantage compounds — over 3-5 years, the lawyer who uses AI closes more matters, bills more hours (at the same rate), and builds a deeper client roster. The lawyer who doesn't gets slowly outcompeted on speed and cost.
What's the worst AI tool you've reviewed?
Anything that costs more than $500/mo and calls itself an "AI-powered practice management platform" but is really a CRM with a ChatGPT plugin stapled on. If the demo is "ask our AI anything about your practice" and the answer is generic, walk away. The bar to be useful in legal AI is high because the privilege and accuracy stakes are real — most startups don't clear it.
